RESEARCH PROTOCOL

1. Research Title

Patterns of Polypharmacy and Multimorbidity Among Adults: A Pharmacoepidemiological Analysis Using NHANES Public Data

2. Background

Polypharmacy refers to the concurrent use of multiple medications, commonly defined as the use of five or more medications. Hyperpolypharmacy is commonly defined as the concurrent use of ten or more medications.

The increasing prevalence of chronic diseases has contributed to the use of multiple medications among adults, particularly older individuals and people living with multiple chronic conditions.

Although multiple medications may be clinically necessary, their use can increase medication burden, the risk of adverse drug events, potential drug–drug interactions and challenges associated with medication management.

Pharmacoepidemiological research helps identify patterns of medication utilization and explore factors associated with polypharmacy in populations.

The National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) provides publicly available health and medication information that can be used to investigate these patterns.

3. Research Problem

Adults with multiple chronic conditions may require several prescription medications. Understanding the distribution of medication use, polypharmacy and multimorbidity is important for evaluating medication burden and identifying populations that may benefit from improved medication review.

4. Aim

To investigate patterns of prescription medication use, polypharmacy and multimorbidity among adults using publicly available NHANES data.

5. Objectives

1. To estimate the prevalence of prescription medication use.
2. To determine the prevalence of polypharmacy and hyperpolypharmacy.
3. To identify the distribution of chronic conditions among adults.
4. To investigate the relationship between age, sex, multimorbidity and medication burden.
5. To explore commonly used therapeutic medication classes.
6. To identify potential opportunities for medication safety assessment.

6. Research Questions

1. What proportion of adults use prescription medications?
2. How common are polypharmacy and hyperpolypharmacy?
3. Which demographic groups have higher medication burden?
4. Is multimorbidity associated with increased medication use?
5. Which therapeutic medication classes are frequently represented?
6. What additional medication safety assessments can be performed using available data?

7. Study Methodology

Study Design

Cross-sectional observational study using secondary analysis of publicly available NHANES data.

Data Source

National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), conducted by the National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS), Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).

Official website: https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/

Study Population

Adult NHANES participants aged 18 years and above.

Data Components

- Demographics (DEMO)
- Medical Conditions (MCQ)
- Prescription Medications (RX)

Participant Identification

The NHANES participant identification variable SEQN will be used to link the relevant datasets.

8. Study Variables

Demographic Variables

- Age
- Sex
- Race and ethnicity

Clinical Variables

- Chronic medical conditions
- Number of chronic conditions
- Multimorbidity status

Medication Variables

- Prescription medication use
- Medication names
- Medication identifiers
- Number of prescription medications
- Polypharmacy category

9. Operational Definitions

- No medication use: No prescription medication use reported during the survey reference period.
- Non-polypharmacy: Use of 1–4 prescription medications.
- Polypharmacy: Use of 5–9 prescription medications.
- Hyperpolypharmacy: Use of 10 or more prescription medications.
- Multimorbidity: Presence of two or more chronic conditions, based on a predefined list of eligible conditions.

Definitions will be finalized after checking NHANES variable documentation and the medication reference period.

10. Statistical Analysis

The analysis will include:

- Descriptive statistics.
- Frequencies and percentages.
- Mean and standard deviation or median and interquartile range, as appropriate.
- Prevalence estimation with confidence intervals.
- Comparison of medication burden across demographic and clinical groups.
- Appropriate tests of association.
- Regression analysis, where justified by the data and study design.

NHANES survey weights, strata and primary sampling units will be considered when conducting population-level analyses.

12. Ethical Considerations

This project uses publicly available, de-identified NHANES data.

The applicable NHANES data-use requirements and institutional research policies will be followed. Any additional approval requirements will be verified before dissemination.

13. Expected Outcomes

The project aims to produce:

1. Estimates of prescription medication use.
2. Polypharmacy and hyperpolypharmacy distributions.
3. A description of multimorbidity patterns.
4. Analysis of demographic and clinical factors associated with medication burden.
5. Tables and figures presenting the findings.
6. A scientific research report and manuscript draft.

14. Limitations

- Cross-sectional data cannot establish causal relationships.
- Medication information may be affected by recall and reporting limitations.
- Prescription medication records may not fully represent actual medication consumption.
- Some clinical and medication safety variables may not be available.
- Potentially inappropriate medication use and drug–drug interactions will only be assessed if sufficient information and appropriate validated criteria are available.
- NHANES survey design must be considered for nationally representative estimates.
